\textbf{Question}:\\
If a line makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the positive direction of the X-axis, $120^\circ$  with the positive direction of the Y-axis, then find the angle which it makes with the positive direction of the Z-axis

\textbf{Solution:} \\
Let the direction (unit) vector of the line be
\begin{align}  
d = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{bmatrix}.
\end{align}

Condition with $x$-axis:
\begin{align}
d^T e_x &= \cos 30^\circ 
\end{align}

\begin{align}
\begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} &= \cos 30^\circ 
\end{align}

\begin{align}
a &= \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} 
\end{align}

Condition with $y$-axis:
\begin{align}
d^T e_y &= \cos 120^\circ 
\end{align}


\begin{align}
\begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} &= \cos 120^\circ 
\end{align}


\begin{align}
b &= -\tfrac{1}{2} 
\end{align}

Now using normalization:
\begin{align}
d^T d &= 1 
\end{align}

\begin{align}
\begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{bmatrix} &= 1 
\end{align}


\begin{align}
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 &= 1 \\
\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 + \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + c^2 &= 1 \\
\tfrac{3}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4} + c^2 &= 1 
\end{align}


\begin{align}
c^2 &= 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad c = 0
\end{align}

Hence,
\begin{align}
d = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[6pt] -\tfrac{1}{2} \\[6pt] 0 \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}


Angle with $z$-axis:
\begin{align}
d^T e_z &= \cos \theta 
\end{align}

\begin{align}
\begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\tfrac{1}{2} & 0 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} &= \cos \theta 
\end{align}
\begin{align}
0 &= \cos \theta 
\end{align}

\begin{align}
\theta &= 90^\circ
\end{align}

Therefore, the line makes angles of $30^\circ$, $120^\circ$, and $90^\circ$ with the $x$-, $y$-, and $z$-axes, respectively.
