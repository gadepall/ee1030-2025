\textbf{Question}:\\
For what value of $k$ $(k>0)$ is the area of the triangle with vertices $(-2,6)$, $(2k,4)$ and $(2k+1,10)$ equal to $52$ sq. units?

\textbf{Solution}:\\
Vertices of triangle are

\begin{align}
\vec{A} = \myvec{-2 \\ 6}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\vec{B} = \myvec{2k \\ 4}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\vec{C} = \myvec{2k+1 \\ 10}
\end{align}

\begin{align}
\vec{A}-\vec{B} 
= \myvec{-2-2k \\ 6-4} = \myvec{-2-2k \\ 2}
\end{align}


\begin{align}
\vec{A} - \vec{C} = \myvec{-2-(2k+1) \\ 6-10} = \myvec{-2k-3 \\-4 }
\end{align}



\begin{align}
(\triangle ABC)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{(\vec{A}-\vec{B}) \times (\vec{A}-\vec{C})}
\end{align}

\begin{align}
(\triangle ABC)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{\myvec{-2-2k \\ 2} \times \myvec{-2k-3 \\ -4}}=\frac{1}{2} |12k + 14|
\end{align}
Given area of triangle = 52 sq.units, \\
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{2}|12k+14| &= 52 \\[6pt]
|12k+14| &= 104.
\end{align}
Now consider the two cases from the absolute value,

\textbf{Case 1:}
\begin{align}
12k+14 &= 104 \\
12k &= 90 \\
k &= \frac{90}{12} = \frac{15}{2}.
\end{align}

\textbf{Case 2:}
\begin{align}
12k+14 &= -104 \\
12k &= -118 \\
k &= -\frac{118}{12} = -\frac{59}{6}.
\end{align}

Because the problem requires \(k>0\), we discard the negative solution.
\begin{center}
$\boxed{k = \dfrac{15}{2}}$
\end{center}
