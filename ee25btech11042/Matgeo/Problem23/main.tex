	\textbf{Question}:\\
	The determinant
	\begin{align}
		\mydet{1+b & b & 1 \\ b & 1+b & 1 \\ 1 & 2b & 1}
	\end{align}
	evaluates to
	\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
		\item $0$
		\item $2b(b-1)$
		\item $2(1-b)(1+2b)$
		\item $3b(1+b)$
	\end{enumerate}
	
	\solution
	
	Let the determinant of the given matrix be $\Delta$.
	\begin{align}
		\Delta = \mydet{1+b & b & 1 \\ b & 1+b & 1 \\ 1 & 2b & 1}
	\end{align}
	We will use row operations to simplify the determinant. Applying the operations $R_1 \to R_1 - R_3$ and $R_2 \to R_2 - R_3$ does not change the value of the determinant.
	
	First, applying $R_1 \to R_1 - R_3$:
	\begin{align}
		\Delta = \mydet{1+b-1 & b-2b & 1-1 \\ b & 1+b & 1 \\ 1 & 2b & 1} = \mydet{b & -b & 0 \\ b & 1+b & 1 \\ 1 & 2b & 1}
	\end{align}
	Next, applying $R_2 \to R_2 - R_3$:
	\begin{align}
		\Delta = \mydet{b & -b & 0 \\ b-1 & 1+b-2b & 1-1 \\ 1 & 2b & 1} = \mydet{b & -b & 0 \\ b-1 & 1-b & 0 \\ 1 & 2b & 1}
	\end{align}
	Now, we apply the column operation $C_1 \to C_1 + C_2$. This operation does not change the value of the determinant.
	\begin{align}
		\Delta = \mydet{b+(-b) & -b & 0 \\ (b-1)+(1-b) & 1-b & 0 \\ 1+2b & 2b & 1} = \mydet{0 & -b & 0 \\ 0 & 1-b & 0 \\ 1+2b & 2b & 1}
	\end{align}
	Since the first column of the matrix has two zero entries, we can easily evaluate the determinant by expanding along the first column:
	\begin{align}
		\Delta &= 0 \mydet{1-b & 0 \\ 2b & 1} - 0 \mydet{-b & 0 \\ 2b & 1} + (1+2b) \mydet{-b & 0 \\ 1-b & 0} \\
		&= (1+2b) ((-b)(0) - (0)(1-b)) \\
		&= (1+2b) (0) = 0
	\end{align}
	The determinant is $0$, which corresponds to option (a).
