\textbf{Question}:\newline
Let $\vec{a} = \hat{\vec{i}} + 2\hat{\vec{j}} + \hat{\vec{k}}$, $\vec{b} = \hat{\vec{i}} - \hat{\vec{j}} + \hat{\vec{k}}$ and $\vec{c} = \hat{\vec{i}} + \hat{\vec{j}} - \hat{\vec{k}}$. A vector in the plane of $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ whose projection on $\vec{c}$ is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, is
\\
\textbf{Solution: }

Given
\begin{align}
\vec{a}=\myvec{1\\2\\1},\vec{b}=\myvec{1\\-1\\1},\vec{c}=\myvec{1\\1\\-1}.
\end{align}

Let $\vec{v}$ be the vector in the plane of $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$
\begin{align}
 [\vec{v} \quad \vec{a} \quad \vec{b}]=0 \implies \vec{v}=\alpha\vec {a}+\beta\vec {b} \\
 \vec{v}=\alpha\myvec{1\\2\\1}+\beta\myvec{1\\-1\\1}
=\myvec{\alpha+\beta\\2\alpha-\beta\\\alpha+\beta}.
 \end{align}
Mathematically, the projection of $\vec{v}$ on $\vec{c}$ is defined as 
\begin{align}
\vec{d}=k\vec{c},\text{such that} \brak{\vec{v}-\vec{d}}^{\top}\vec{d}=0 
\end{align}
yielding
\begin{align}
\brak{\vec{v}-k\vec{c}}^{\top}\vec{d}=0 
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\text{or},
k=\frac{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{c}}{\|\vec{c\|^{2}}}, \implies
\vec{d}=\frac{\vec v^{\top}\vec{c}}{\|\vec{c}\|^{2}}\;\vec{c}.  
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\norm{\vec{c}}^2=\vec{c}^\top\vec{c}&=\myvec{1&1&-1}\myvec{1\\1\\-1} \\
&=1+1+1=3  \implies \norm{\vec{c}}=\sqrt{3}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{c}&=\myvec{\alpha+\beta & 2\alpha-\beta & \alpha+\beta}\myvec{1\\1\\-1} \\
&=\alpha+\beta + 2\alpha-\beta - \alpha-\beta=2\alpha-\beta
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\vec{d}=\frac{2\alpha-\beta}{3}\vec{c}
\end{align}
Given $\norm{d}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ , Then 
\begin{align}
\frac{|2\alpha-\beta|}{3}\norm{\vec{c}}=\frac{|2\alpha-\beta|}{3}\sqrt{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}  \implies |2\alpha - \beta| = 1 
\end{align}
From equation(3) we know that that $x$ and $z$ coordinates in our required vector must be equal. \\
If $2\alpha - \beta $ = -1,from the most reliable option i.e option(1) which has the same $x$ and $z$ coordinates, we get 
\begin{align}
    \alpha+\beta=4\ \text{and}\ 2\alpha-\beta=-1 
\end{align}
On adding gives 
\begin{align}
    3\alpha=3 \implies \alpha =1\ \text{and}\ \beta=3 
    \end{align}
    \begin{align}
    \vec{v}=\myvec{4 \\ -1\\ 4}
    \end{align}
If $2\alpha -\beta $ =1,from the options $x$ and $z$ coordinates are
not equal,No option satisfy.
