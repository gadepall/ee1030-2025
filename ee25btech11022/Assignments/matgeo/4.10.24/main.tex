\textbf{Question}:Find the equations of the lines through the point of intersection of the line  $x - y + 1 = 0$ and $2x - 3y + 5 = 0$ and whose distance from the point $\brak{3, 2}$ is $\frac{7}{5}$. 

\textbf{Solution}:
Given lines are 
\begin{align}
\myvec{ 1 & -1 } \myvec{ x \\ y } + 1 = 0 ,
\myvec{ 2 & -3 } \myvec{ x \\ y } + 5 = 0 
\end{align}
Cramer's rule: 
\begin{align}
\Delta = \mydet{1 & -1 \\ 2 & -3} = -3+2 = -1 \\
x = \frac{\mydet{-1 & -1 \\ -5 & -3}}{\Delta} = \frac{3-5}{-1} = 2\\
y = \frac{\mydet{1 & -1 \\ 2 & -5}}{\Delta} = \frac{-5+2}{-1} =3 
\end{align}
Intersection of given two lines is $\myvec{2 \\ 3}$ \\
General line through $\brak{x_0,y_0}$ with normal vector \myvec{a \\ b} is
\begin{align}
\myvec{a & b}\brak{\myvec{x \\ y} -\myvec{2 \\ 3}} = 0 
\end{align}
Distance from point $\myvec{3 \\ 2}$  to line is
\begin{align}
Distance = \frac{\lvert\myvec{a & b}\brak{\myvec{3 \\ 2}-\myvec{2 \\ 3}}\rvert}{\sqrt{\myvec{a & b}\myvec{a \\ b}}} =\frac{7}{5} \\
\frac{\lvert\myvec{a & b}\brak{\myvec{3-2 \\ 2-3}-\myvec{2 \\ 3}}\rvert}{\sqrt{
\myvec{a & b}\myvec{a \\ b}}} =\frac{7}{5} \\
\frac{|a - b|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} = \frac{7}{5}
\end{align}
Let t = $\frac{a}{b}$ \quad \brak{b \neq 0} \\
\begin{align}
\frac{|t - 1|}{\sqrt{t^2 + 1}} = \frac{7}{5} 
\end{align}
Squaring  on both sides
\begin{align}
25 (t - 1)^2 &= 49 (t^2 + 1) \\
25 (t^2 - 2 t + 1) &= 49 t^2 + 49 \\
25 t^2 - 50 t + 25 &= 49 t^2 + 49 \\
24 t^2 + 50 t + 24 &= 0 \\
12 t^2 + 25 t + 12 &= 0 \\
t = \frac{-25\pm \sqrt{25^2-4\times12\times12}}{2\times12} =\frac{-25\pm 7}{24} 
\end{align}
Solutions \quad $t_1 = -\frac{3}{4},t_2 = -1$ \\
Normal vectors \brak{let b=1} :
\begin{align}
\myvec{a \\ b} =\myvec{\frac{-3}{4} \\ 1},\myvec{-1 \\ 1}
\end{align}
Equation constants \quad c = -a\brak{2}-b\brak{3} 

\begin{align}
c_1 =\brak{-2}\times\brak{-\frac{3}{4}} -3\times1 =\frac{3}{2} -3 =\frac{-3}{2} \\
c_2 = \brak{-2}\times\brak{-1} - 3\times1 = 2 -3= -1 
\end{align}

required equations of line are  

\begin{align}
\myvec{\frac{-3}{4} & 1} \myvec{x \\ y} -\frac{3}{2} = 0 \\
\myvec{-1 & 1} \myvec{x \\ y} - 1 = 0 
\end{align}
