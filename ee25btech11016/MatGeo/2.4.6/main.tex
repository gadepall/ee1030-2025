\textbf{Question}:\\
If $\vec{a} = 5\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$ and $\vec{b} = \hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}$, then show that the vectors $\vec{a} + \vec{b}$ and $\vec{a} - \vec{b}$ are perpendicular. \\

\solution \\

Two vectors are perpendicular if their dot product is zero. We need to show that $(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})^\top (\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) = 0$.

First, we find the vectors $\vec{a} + \vec{b}$ and $\vec{a} - \vec{b}$.
The given vectors can be written in column form as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
Now, we calculate the sum and difference:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 5+1 \\ -1+3 \\ -3-5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 2 \\ -8 \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 5-1 \\ -1-3 \\ -3-(-5) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
Next, we compute the dot product of these two resultant vectors:
\begin{align}
(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})^\top (\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}) &= \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 2 & -8 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \nonumber \\
&= (6)(4) + (2)(-4) + (-8)(2) \nonumber \\
&= 24 - 8 - 16 \nonumber \\
&= 0
\end{align}
Since the dot product is zero, the vectors $(\vec{a} + \vec{b})$ and $(\vec{a} - \vec{b})$ are perpendicular. Hence, proved.
